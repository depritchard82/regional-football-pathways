CITIZENSHIP, RESIDENCE AND FOOTBALL

Passports change part of the picture.

A child can hold more than one nationality. Consider all citizenships already held, their usual home and any existing UK permission. An “English passport” normally means a passport showing British citizen; other British nationality categories can have different rights.

These selections explain research scenarios. They are not stored and do not determine eligibility. Citizenship already held is different from an application awaiting approval; check the documents needed to prove it before travel.

What should we tell the club?
  • All citizenships already held and any applications still pending.
  • Usual residence, age, proposed dates and any confirmed UK immigration status.
  • Current and previous clubs, countries of registration, and whether this would be a first registration.
  • Whether the enquiry concerns a short trial, education programme or longer-term registration.

Do not send passport scans in an initial unsolicited enquiry. First establish the official club contact and its secure document process.

Ask the club’s association

Registration procedures vary between the four associations. Some clubs play across borders; ask the club which association handles its registration.

01 / RECOMMENDED PREPARATION

A clear, evidence-based football CV.

A one- or two-page PDF is a useful starting point. Prioritise readable facts, working video links and accurate competition context over elaborate design.

  • Player: name, date of birth for the private application, primary and secondary positions, preferred foot. Add dated height/weight measurements where requested.
  • Location and contact: Mackay or home town, Queensland, Australia. For under-18s, use a parent/guardian contact with country code. Follow the club's communication instructions.
  • Current football: club, team, exact competition/division, age group and season. State junior or senior football. Explain unfamiliar Australian competitions without inventing a UK equivalent.
  • History and evidence: recent clubs and dates, representative selections, verified appearances/minutes and relevant achievements. Give the period and competition for statistics; omit unverified numbers.
  • Playing profile: three or four specific strengths, supported by match examples. For example: “Receives under pressure and switches play — video 00:45 and 01:30.” Avoid unsupported ratings such as “Premier League standard”.
  • Supporting links: highlights, full matches and referees who have agreed to be contacted.
  • Overseas circumstances: citizenship already held, pending applications separately, usual residence, known UK status and proposed dates. A Maltese or other passport is not itself a verdict on UK football eligibility.

Keep passport scans, full home address, medical records and other sensitive documents out of a public profile. Share specific documents only through the receiving club's verified process when needed.

02 / RECOMMENDED PREPARATION

References that explain the player.

Prepare one strong football reference; add a second if it provides a different perspective. No universal two-reference rule or coaching-licence requirement was identified in the sources reviewed.

Who?What they can contribute
Current team coachMatch performance, consistency, reliability and teamwork over time.
Former or representative coachA substantial period of direct observation or experience in another competition.
Position-specific coachTechnical detail, particularly for goalkeepers. Explain whether observations are from training or matches.
Teacher / school sport coordinatorEducation, character and pastoral context where relevant; supplementary to football assessment.

A local development coach can be a useful referee. The strength of the reference comes from direct knowledge and specific evidence. A parent-only endorsement or generic promotional statement provides less independent assessment.

Ask the referee to include

  • Name, role, organisation, relevant qualifications if held, and agreed contact details.
  • How long they have coached the player, how often, and in what match/training setting.
  • Positions and competition level; two or three strengths with examples.
  • Decision-making, off-ball contribution, response to pressure, coachability and interaction with teammates.
  • Development priorities and a balanced assessment of current ability.
  • Date, signature and permission for the club to contact them.
“I have coached [player] at [club/programme] since [date], observing them in [training and match context]. They primarily play [position] in [competition]. Their strengths include [attributes], demonstrated by [specific examples].”

Give the referee a factual CV and the intended purpose. They should write or approve the final assessment. Do not invent a signature, qualification or endorsement.

03 / RECOMMENDED PREPARATION

Show decisions, not just spectacular moments.

Start with a three- to five-minute highlights video, with the strongest examples early. This draws on Hudl's recruiting guidance and Veo's soccer guidance. Both discuss US recruiting: this is practical presentation advice, not a UK requirement. Follow a club's stated duration or exercise instead.

  1. Collect recent match footage. Confirm permission to record, edit and share with the club, competition and footage owner. Keep original files.
  2. Log evidence. Record date, opponent, competition, shirt number, match timestamp and the skill or decision shown.
  3. Identify the player. Use a brief arrow/circle before each clip and state shirt colour/number. Avoid a long introduction.
  4. Preserve context. Show the lead-in, options and outcome. Use a stable, sufficiently wide view at normal speed; avoid distracting music, effects and repeated replays.
  5. Show the role. Include off-ball work and transitions as well as touches. Training clips can supplement matches or satisfy a club-specific request.
  6. Retain full games. Have one or two recent representative matches available with a timestamp index. This number is our recommendation, not a compulsory submission rule.
  7. Test delivery. Use links rather than large email attachments. Check access on another device and as a signed-out viewer, or give the named reviewer the required access. Avoid unexpected paywalls or expired links.
PositionUseful evidence to select
GoalkeeperPositioning, handling, crosses, distribution and decisions outside the penalty area.
Centre-backCovering, positioning, defensive duels and progressing possession.
Full-back / wing-backOne-on-one defending, recovery, supporting attacks and delivery.
MidfielderScanning, receiving under pressure, passing choices, defensive positioning and transitions.
Winger / forwardMovement, combination play, chance creation, pressing and finishing decisions.

For technically strong regional players, show how technique holds up under pressure. Full matches help a reviewer see involvement and decisions that a montage cannot show.

Young players and privacy: follow the club's consent arrangements and Football Australia's safeguarding guidance. Public titles should not expose full birth dates or personal contacts. An unlisted video link can still be forwarded; choose sharing settings deliberately and involve the player and parent/guardian.

04 / SEND WHAT IS RELEVANT

Build the supporting pack in stages.

Initial enquiry

Short introduction, age, pathway, current football, home country, proposed dates and eligibility questions. Include a CV or video only where the published route invites it.

Requested assessment material

Tailored CV, approved reference, footage and fixture list. Include fixture time zone, venue and opposition. Ask whether remote review is possible for an Australian-based player.

Education programme

Prepare school year and academic records if asked. Ask how Australian qualifications, residency and funding are assessed. Do not assume a football offer includes school admission.

After a suitable invitation

Confirm consent, registration history, trial paperwork, supervision, accommodation, expenses, insurance and any requested identity or medical information through verified channels.

For example, Sutton's college pathway publishes school-year, local travel and academic criteria. This is programme-specific evidence, not a standard for all clubs.

Use a simple folder: CV; references; video links and clip log; fixtures; enquiry tracker. Keep sensitive documents separately. Name files clearly, for example PlayerName_FootballCV_2026-10.pdf. Replace outdated versions and tailor the cover email to the correct team.

Follow any published response policy. Southampton discourages repeated contact; Cardiff warns that it cannot reply to every application. Do not interpret silence as an invitation or send bulk repeat messages.

EVIDENCE YOU CAN INSPECT

Know what the
information tells you.

Built for independent families

This resource grew from work with technical young players in Mackay, Queensland. It helps families find clubs and make their own enquiries. It does not arrange tours, represent players or sell access to trials.

What is connected

The directory is generated from the existing club database. This edition contains the 17 October 2026 research snapshot. Database changes appear when a refreshed edition is published; club websites are not monitored live.

The men’s and women’s registers are separate team records, not a count of unique organisations. U19 directory records are not added again here. Older entries may lack an individual check date.

Coverage has limits

A league listing does not prove professional status, a youth team, a vacancy or overseas eligibility. Some contacts are general club offices. The 17 recruitment research records include historical and contact-only guidance.

Season allocations may change. Check the linked primary source and ask the club to confirm its current programme.

Your plan stays with you

The site stores your shortlist, checklist and planning notes in your browser’s local storage. No player account or document upload is required. Do not enter passport numbers, medical details or children’s full personal profiles.

Browser data can be cleared and does not sync between devices. Export a backup. The hosting service may process normal access logs. External sites have their own privacy policies.

Research coverage

Contact routes have not been tested by outreach. No trial invitations or overseas acceptance have been established by this research.
